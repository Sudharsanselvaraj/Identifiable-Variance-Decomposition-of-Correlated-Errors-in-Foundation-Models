\section*{S1. Why per-model variance decomposition is not enough}\label{sec:precision}

\subsection*{Model and identification}
Let $Y_i$ be a per-model trait (e.g., accuracy), with family $f(i)$ and release quarter $e(i)$:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
Y_i &= \mu + \alpha_{f(i)} + \beta_{e(i)} + u_i,\\
\alpha &\sim \mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2_L),\quad \beta \sim \mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2_E),\quad u \sim \mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2_U),
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:vc}
\end{equation}
so that $\operatorname{Var}(\mathbf{y}) = \sigma^2_L Z_F Z_F^\top + \sigma^2_E Z_E Z_E^\top + \sigma^2_U I$, with only the intercept as a fixed effect. The variance components are identified when the covariance basis $\{Z_F Z_F^\top, Z_E Z_E^\top, I\}$ is linearly independent. The rank of the fixed-effects dummy design $[\mathbf{1} \mid Z_F \mid Z_E]$, used as the gate in the earlier version of this work, is not the relevant condition for this model: for the sixteen models evaluated there, that design is rank-deficient (rank 14 of 15) while the covariance basis has full rank (3 of 3). Its condition number also depends on the arbitrary choice of reference level (TABLE~\ref{tab:precision}).

\subsection*{Precision}
Identification does not imply precision. For each design we compute the expected restricted-likelihood information $I_{jk} = \tfrac12\operatorname{tr}(P V_j P V_k)$ at four share scenarios (lineage-dominant 0.5/0.2/0.3, era-dominant 0.2/0.5/0.3, balanced, and low-signal 0.1/0.1/0.8), the delta-method standard error of each share, and, as the verdict, the Monte Carlo root-mean-square error (RMSE) of the share estimates under the same REML estimator (500 replicates per scenario). The analytic standard error tracks the Monte Carlo RMSE closely (e.g., 0.289 versus 0.285 for the sixteen-model design), so it can be used before any measurement.

No design reaches an RMSE of 0.10 for both shares (TABLE~\ref{tab:precision}): not the sixteen evaluated models (0.30), not the 47-model candidate frame (0.19), and not the 30-model configuration the earlier version described as sufficient (0.19). Removing the single model responsible for the fixed-effects rank deficiency leaves the RMSE unchanged (0.302). The binding constraint is the number of \emph{levels} of each factor: more models per level help only up to a point (with six families and fourteen quarters, 22 models give 0.256 and 47 give 0.189). In staggered designs (main paper, Fig.~4), bringing both shares within $\pm 0.10$ requires on the order of sixty families and fourteen quarters; with eight quarters the era share never falls below about 0.14. Because release quarters are few by construction, a per-model decomposition is a poor instrument for the paper's research questions in populations like those examined here. Within the designs and scenarios examined, this motivates the pair-level design; it does not show that the shares are inestimable under every design, only that the required numbers of families and quarters exceed those available.

\section*{S5. Agreement as a signal of shared lineage}
This analysis is post hoc and descriptive. If related models share wrong answers, a pair's agreement should carry information about whether the two share a root. In the primary sample, agreement alone separates same-root from different-root pairs with an area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC) of 0.95, and agreement net of the accuracy and release-gap terms of the pre-specified model with 0.88 (Fig.~\ref{fig:detection}). Above an agreement of 0.7 lie 68\% of same-root pairs but only 2.1\% of different-root pairs. Because different-root pairs outnumber same-root pairs by 85 to 1, however, most pairs above that threshold are still unrelated (about 3{,}600 against 1{,}400), so agreement on one benchmark flags candidate relatives rather than establishing them. AUC here is a descriptive summary, not the accuracy of a validated lineage detector.

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.6\textwidth]{fig_detection.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{Agreement as a signal of shared lineage} (primary sample; 2{,}020 same-root and 171{,}735 different-root pairs; densities). (a) Observed agreement on wrong answers. (b) Agreement after removing the accuracy and release-gap terms of the pre-specified model. AUC: probability that a random same-root pair scores higher than a random different-root pair.}

\subsection*{Item fixed effects, bootstrap and permutation}
The pair-level outcome conditions on items both models get wrong, so a pair of strong models is compared on harder items than a pair of weak models. The pair $\times$ item model regresses the indicator of the same wrong option on the pair covariates over every (pair, item) cell in which both models are wrong, with a fixed effect for each item; it is computed exactly from item-level aggregates and weights each pair by its number of jointly wrong items, so it is shown beside a weighted pair-level fit without item effects. The root cluster bootstrap resamples lineage roots with replacement (999 draws). The permutation test shuffles root labels among the models uploaded in the same quarter, preserving root sizes and the time structure (999 permutations, so the smallest attainable $p$ is 0.001). Across the 999 permutations the largest absolute shared-root coefficient is 1.2 points in the primary sample and 1.4 in the expanded sample. The bootstrap interval is asymmetric because resampling roots sometimes omits the two largest roots, whose coefficients are among the smallest.

\subsection*{Dose-response within roots, and every root counted once}
Parent--child pairs come disproportionately from small roots (82 of 177), and small roots show larger coefficients, so the tree-distance gradient could reflect root composition. The within-root model uses same-root pairs only, with a fixed effect for each root, so each distance class is compared with distance 3+ pairs of the same root. The pre-specified coefficient weights roots by their number of same-root pairs, so the two largest roots dominate it; the last two rows of TABLE~\ref{tab:r3dose} count roots instead.

\subsection*{Simulation check with the real accuracies}

A further scenario has no lineage or release-time effect, but the probability of choosing the common distractor rises with ability ($0.05 + 0.5\,\mathrm{acc}^2$), so agreement depends non-linearly on accuracy. \section*{S7. Record of the earlier per-model evaluation}
This section documents an evaluation that preceded the pair-level design. It is reported as an audit record. None of its measurements enters any result of the main paper or of Sections~S3--S6, and it is not a second empirical study. Section~S1 uses only the family and quarter labels of its sixteen models to define one simulated design (the point labelled 16 in Fig.~4 of the main paper).

\subsection*{Selection and plan}
A 47-model candidate frame was reduced to a 22-model roster by a design gate that used only family $\times$ quarter occupancy, declared lineage edges, identifiability constraints and estimated cost, never measured traits (\texttt{datasets/coverage/minimum\_valid\_population.csv}, \texttt{g3\_report.md}; committed 3 August 2026, last revised 5 August). The two DeepSeek models of the roster (671B and 685B mixture-of-experts) did not fit the available compute; three re-gate variants that excluded them were infeasible because the gate then required other models of the same size class, and a rule to complete their traits by model-based multiple imputation (five draws, seed 2026) was recorded on 16 August 2026, before the first evaluation. The run plan, recorded the same day and also before the first evaluation, assigned the remaining 20 models to one rented NVIDIA A100 80\,GB (SXM4) GPU: 17 in bfloat16 and Qwen1.5-72B, Llama-3.1-70B and Llama-3.3-70B in 4-bit NF4, with MMLU 5-shot log-likelihood scoring on all 14{,}042 items through \texttt{lm-eval} 0.4.12 and \texttt{transformers} 4.57.6 (\texttt{docs/05\_Experiments/Exp02\_GPU\_Runbook.md}). The planned resources were a GPU disk of at least 300\,GB and about 604 GPU-minutes, estimated at USD 16--20 at about USD 1.60 per hour. These are planned values: no pod configuration, storage record or invoice is in the repository, so the attached disk, run time and cost actually incurred are not reported.

\subsection*{What was recorded}
Between 16 and 18 August 2026, results for 16 models were committed, one model per commit (\texttt{datasets/phase2\_eval\_results.csv}, \texttt{datasets/eval\_samples/}). TABLE~\ref{tab:runpod} compares them with the plan. Findings:
\begin{itemize}
\item Results for Qwen1.5-72B, Llama-3.1-70B, Llama-3.3-70B and Phi-4-reasoning-vision-15B were not run: the authors stopped the evaluation after 16 models because further GPU time could not be funded. No decision-log entry records the stop. None of the three models planned in 4-bit was therefore run.
\item Mistral-Small-4, planned as a 32B model in bfloat16, was recorded from the repository \texttt{Mistral-Small-4-119B-2603} in 4-bit. No decision-log entry records the change; the reproducibility checklist of the earlier version notes only that the 119B model does not fit in bfloat16 on an 80\,GB GPU. Five further models were recorded from repositories other than those in the plan.
\item Every per-question file stores one log-likelihood per item and predicts option~A on all 14{,}042 items: the extraction code read only the first option's score. The error was found and fixed in the code on 9 October 2026. No raw \texttt{lm-eval} output with all four scores per item was found in the repository, its full history (including stashes and unreachable commits) or the computer used for the analysis, so the per-question predictions cannot be reconstructed.
\item The recorded accuracies are \texttt{lm-eval}'s own aggregates and do not pass through the faulty extraction, but they cannot be validated item by item. Qwen-7B's value equals the share of items whose correct answer is~A; the leaderboard's item-validated predictions give 0.584 for the same repository. Five other models score at chance level (0.23--0.25) for reasons not established, including three 24B instruction models. Mistral-Small-3.1 and 3.2 were re-run after their first results, following a loader change that registered their architecture (Mistral3) with the causal-language-model loader (commit \texttt{cca711c}; an earlier fix to quantization arguments and rotary-position parameters, \texttt{27886ef}, preceded both runs), and remained at chance (0.249 and 0.250 before, 0.234 and 0.231 after). Phi-1.5, the only other model also in the validated leaderboard data, agrees with it (0.422 against 0.424).
\item The DeepSeek imputation was implemented but never executed on measured data.
\end{itemize}
No recorded measurement is therefore used. Completing this evaluation, which the authors do not plan to do, would require re-running all twenty planned models with the corrected extraction and resolving the chance-level results.
