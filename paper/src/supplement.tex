\section*{S1. Why per-model variance decomposition is not enough}\label{sec:precision}

\subsection*{Model and identification}
Let $Y_i$ be a per-model trait (e.g., accuracy), with family $f(i)$ and release quarter $e(i)$:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
Y_i &= \mu + \alpha_{f(i)} + \beta_{e(i)} + u_i,\\
\alpha &\sim \mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2_L),\quad \beta \sim \mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2_E),\quad u \sim \mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2_U),
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:vc}
\end{equation}
so that $\operatorname{Var}(\mathbf{y}) = \sigma^2_L Z_F Z_F^\top + \sigma^2_E Z_E Z_E^\top + \sigma^2_U I$, with only the intercept as a fixed effect. The variance components are identified when the covariance basis $\{Z_F Z_F^\top, Z_E Z_E^\top, I\}$ is linearly independent. The rank of the fixed-effects dummy design $[\mathbf{1} \mid Z_F \mid Z_E]$, used as the gate in the earlier version of this work, is not the relevant condition for this model: for the sixteen models evaluated there, that design is rank-deficient (rank 14 of 15) while the covariance basis has full rank (3 of 3). Its condition number also depends on the arbitrary choice of reference level (TABLE~\ref{tab:precision}).

\subsection*{Precision}
Identification does not imply precision. For each design we compute the expected restricted-likelihood information $I_{jk} = \tfrac12\operatorname{tr}(P V_j P V_k)$ at four share scenarios (lineage-dominant 0.5/0.2/0.3, era-dominant 0.2/0.5/0.3, balanced, and low-signal 0.1/0.1/0.8), the delta-method standard error of each share, and, as the verdict, the Monte Carlo root-mean-square error (RMSE) of the share estimates under the same REML estimator (500 replicates per scenario). The analytic standard error approximates the Monte Carlo RMSE (e.g., 0.289 versus 0.285 for the family share of the sixteen-model design in the lineage-dominant scenario) but overstates it in the low-signal scenario (era share: 0.411 versus 0.275), so it is a conservative screen before any measurement and the Monte Carlo RMSE is the verdict.

No design reaches an RMSE of 0.10 for both shares (TABLE~\ref{tab:precision}): not the sixteen evaluated models (0.30), not the 47-model candidate frame (0.19), and not the 30-model configuration the earlier version described as sufficient (0.19). Removing the single model responsible for the fixed-effects rank deficiency leaves the RMSE unchanged (0.302). The binding constraint is the number of \emph{levels} of each factor: more models per level help only up to a point (with six families and fourteen quarters, 22 models give 0.256 and 47 give 0.189). In staggered designs (Fig.~\ref{fig:scaling}), bringing both shares within $\pm 0.10$ requires on the order of sixty families and fourteen quarters; with eight quarters the era share never falls below about 0.14. Because release quarters are few by construction, a per-model decomposition is a poor instrument for the paper's research questions in populations like those examined here. Within the designs and scenarios examined, this motivates the pair-level design; it does not show that the shares are inestimable under every design, only that the required numbers of families and quarters exceed those available.

\section*{S5. Agreement as a signal of shared lineage}
This analysis is post hoc and descriptive. If related models share wrong answers, a pair's agreement should carry information about whether the two share a root. In the primary sample, agreement alone separates same-root from different-root pairs with an area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC) of 0.95, and agreement net of the accuracy and release-gap terms of the pre-specified model with 0.88 (Fig.~\ref{fig:detection}). Above an agreement of 0.7 lie 68\% of same-root pairs but only 2.1\% of different-root pairs. Because different-root pairs outnumber same-root pairs by 85 to 1, however, most pairs above that threshold are still unrelated (about 3{,}600 against 1{,}400), so agreement on one benchmark flags candidate relatives rather than establishing them. AUC here is a descriptive summary, not the accuracy of a validated lineage detector.

\subsection*{Flexible accuracy control and answer-key errors}
The pre-specified model controls accuracy through the linear sum and absolute difference of the two models' accuracies. TABLE~\ref{tab:r3} replaces them with a full quadratic surface, with fixed effects for every unordered pair of accuracy bins (deciles: 55 cells; 20 bins: 210 cells), and with the subset of pairs whose accuracies differ by at most 0.02 (plus decile cells). The answer-key check matches 5{,}698 of the 14{,}042 items to MMLU-Redux 2.0, which re-annotates 100 items per subject; 369 of the matched items are annotated as erroneous (wrong ground truth 106, no correct answer 36, several correct answers 39, unclear question or options 156, expert knowledge needed 32) and 5{,}329 as correct. Accuracy is recomputed on each item subset.

\textbf{Accuracy-matched pairs (fourth review).} The matched analysis gives a positive same-month coefficient ($+3.3$ points, primary). Of its 28{,}170 primary pairs, 74\% join two models below 0.30 accuracy and the median pair accuracy is 0.26; above 0.30 accuracy 7{,}404 pairs remain (median pair accuracy 0.63, 96\% with at least one 2024 upload). The coefficient is $+3.4$ points among them (jackknife CI $-0.6$ to $+7.4$), $+3.1$ ($+1.6$ to $+4.7$) in the primary sample with the S1 option-distribution covariate added, $+4.0$ ($+0.9$ to $+7.1$) with S1 above 0.30 accuracy and $+3.3$ ($+0.9$ to $+5.7$) in the expanded sample with S1. With S1 in the expanded sample above 0.30 accuracy the leave-one-root fits are singular and the jackknife interval is not usable. The shared-root coefficient stays between 11.7 and 14.9 points in these fits. Script: \texttt{scripts/run\_exp04\_matched\_followup.py}; output \texttt{results/exp04\_revision3/R13.csv}.

\subsection*{Item fixed effects, bootstrap and permutation}
The pair-level outcome conditions on items both models get wrong, so a pair of strong models is compared on harder items than a pair of weak models. The pair $\times$ item model regresses the indicator of the same wrong option on the pair covariates over every (pair, item) cell in which both models are wrong, with a fixed effect for each item; it is computed exactly from item-level aggregates and weights each pair by its number of jointly wrong items, so it is shown beside a weighted pair-level fit without item effects. The root cluster bootstrap resamples lineage roots with replacement (999 draws) and keeps every pair of distinct models among the drawn copies, so a pair from a root drawn $k$ times enters $k^2$ times and remains a same-root pair, and a pair spanning roots drawn $k$ and $m$ times enters $km$ times (the vertex, or pigeonhole, bootstrap for dyadic data applied to roots; the resampling is checked against explicit enumeration in the test suite). The permutation test shuffles root labels among the models uploaded in the same quarter, preserving root sizes and the time structure (999 permutations, so the smallest attainable $p$ is 0.001). Across the 999 permutations the largest absolute shared-root coefficient is 1.2 points in the primary sample and 1.4 in the expanded sample. The bootstrap interval is asymmetric because resampling roots sometimes omits the two largest roots, whose coefficients are among the smallest.

\subsection*{Execution configuration}
The planned configuration (\texttt{docs/05\_Experiments/Exp02\_GPU\_Runbook.md}, addendum of 16 August 2026) was:
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Hardware.} One RunPod pod with one NVIDIA A100 SXM4 80\,GB GPU, not a cluster. The authors report a host with 16 vCPUs and 117\,GB of RAM; no pod record in the repository confirms it.
\item \emph{Software.} Python~3.11 PyTorch template (PyTorch 2.8.0 as reported by the authors; not recorded); \texttt{lm-eval}~0.4.12; \texttt{transformers}~4.57.6 (pinned because version~5 removed the \texttt{load\_in\_4bit} argument that \texttt{lm-eval}~0.4.12 passes); \texttt{accelerate} and \texttt{bitsandbytes} (versions not pinned or recorded).
\item \emph{Task.} MMLU, 5-shot, log-likelihood scoring of the four answer options (no generation), on the same 14{,}042 items for every model.
\item \emph{Model arguments.} \texttt{--dtype bfloat16}, \texttt{--attn sdpa}, \texttt{--batch auto}; \texttt{--quant none} for the 17 models planned in bfloat16 and \texttt{--quant 4bit} for the three 70B-class models; \texttt{max\_length=2048,truncation=True} for Phi-1, Phi-1.5 and Phi-2; \texttt{trust\_remote\_code} where the repository requires it. The code's 4-bit path passes only \texttt{load\_in\_4bit=True}, which selects the \texttt{bitsandbytes} default 4-bit type (FP4), not the NF4 type named in the plan. A loader patch in force during the runs removed quantization arguments for one Mistral model class, so a run tagged 4-bit may not have been quantized as tagged.
\item \emph{Procedure.} One model per invocation; check that 14{,}042 samples were scored; commit the result row and the per-question file; delete the model cache, so that the committed files are the durable record if the pod is reclaimed; previously completed models are skipped on resumption. A pilot (Phi-2, 100 items) failed before any measurement and wrote no data.
\item \emph{Intake check.} \texttt{lineage\_era.phase2\_eval\_check} with the 20-model manifest (\texttt{datasets/coverage/a100\_full\_subset.csv}) was to pass before any analysis. It requires one row per planned model, accuracy values in range, per-question row counts equal to the scored samples, valid correctness flags, a common item set, and, since the October 2026 fix, more than one log-likelihood per item and non-constant predictions. No passing run is recorded. Run on the committed outputs on 10 October 2026 it fails with 34 errors: four planned models missing; \texttt{acc\_norm} empty for all sixteen rows; and, for every one of the sixteen per-question files, a constant prediction and a single log-likelihood per item. Row counts (14{,}042), correctness flags and the common item set pass.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Resource provenance}
The plan specified a GPU disk of at least 300\,GB (the 70B models were to be downloaded in bfloat16 before 4-bit loading, about 140\,GB each), about 604 GPU-minutes (about 10 hours) and an estimated cost of USD 16--20 at about USD 1.60 per hour. These are planned values. The commits of results span 16 August 18:08 to 18 August 09:12 UTC; that is wall-clock time including idle periods, not measured run time. No pod configuration, storage record, event log or invoice is in the repository, so the attached disk, run time and cost actually incurred are not reported.

\subsection*{What was recorded}
Between 16 and 18 August 2026, results for 16 models were committed, one model per commit (\texttt{datasets/phase2\_eval\_results.csv}, \texttt{datasets/eval\_samples/}). TABLE~\ref{tab:runpod} compares them with the plan. These are recorded runs, not validated measurements.
\begin{itemize}
\item Qwen1.5-72B, Llama-3.1-70B, Llama-3.3-70B and Phi-4-reasoning-vision-15B were not run: the authors stopped the evaluation after 16 models because further GPU time could not be funded. No decision-log entry records the stop. None of the three models planned in 4-bit was therefore run.
\item Mistral-Small-4, planned as a 32B model in bfloat16, was recorded from the repository \texttt{Mistral-Small-4-119B-2603} with a 4-bit tag. No decision-log entry records the change; the reproducibility checklist of the earlier version notes only that the 119B model does not fit in bfloat16 on an 80\,GB GPU. Five further models were recorded from repositories other than those in the plan (TABLE~\ref{tab:runpod}).
\item \emph{Integrity of the per-question files.} Every per-question file stores one log-likelihood per item and predicts option~A on all 14{,}042 items, because the extraction code read only the first option's score (\texttt{resps[0]}) instead of one score per option. The error was found and fixed in the code on 9 October 2026. No raw \texttt{lm-eval} output with all four scores per item was found in the repository, its full history (including stashes and unreachable commits) or the computer used for the analysis, so the per-question predictions cannot be reconstructed.
\item \emph{Aggregate accuracies.} The recorded accuracies are \texttt{lm-eval}'s own aggregates and do not pass through the faulty extraction, but they cannot be validated item by item. Qwen-7B's value equals the share of items whose correct answer is~A; the leaderboard's item-validated predictions give 0.584 for the same repository. Five other models score at chance level (0.23--0.25) for reasons not established, including three 24B instruction models. Mistral-Small-3.1 and 3.2 were re-run after their first results, following a loader change that registered their architecture (Mistral3) with the causal-language-model loader (commit \texttt{cca711c}; an earlier fix to quantization arguments and rotary-position parameters, \texttt{27886ef}, preceded both runs), and remained at chance (0.249 and 0.250 before, 0.234 and 0.231 after). Phi-1.5, the only other model also in the validated leaderboard data, agrees with it (0.422 against 0.424).
\item \emph{Imputation.} The DeepSeek imputation was implemented but never executed on measured data, so the planned 22-entry roster was never completed.
\end{itemize}
No recorded measurement is therefore used. Completing this evaluation, which the authors do not plan to do, would require re-running all twenty planned models with the corrected extraction and resolving the chance-level results.

\section*{S8. Lineage-resolution algorithm}
Algorithm~\ref{alg:lineage} gives the exact rules used to resolve each model's declared parent chain to a lineage root (main paper, Section~III-B).

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Lineage root of a model}
\label{alg:lineage}
\begin{algsteps}
\item $n \leftarrow$ canonical Hub identifier of the model (renames collapsed); $d \leftarrow 0$
\item \algkw{while} $d < 20$:
\item \algind{1}\algkw{if} $n$ declares several parents: \algkw{return} unresolved (merge)
\item \algind{1}$p \leftarrow$ declared parent of $n$ (expanded population only: at $d = 0$, the configuration link if the card declares none)
\item \algind{1}\algkw{if} $d > 0$ and $n$ no longer exists: \algkw{return} unresolved (missing ancestor)
\item \algind{1}\algkw{if} $p$ is none and $n$ is a listed re-upload: $p \leftarrow$ the checkpoint it copies
\item \algind{1}\algkw{if} $p$ is none: \algkw{if} the leaderboard lists $n$ as a fine-tune, \algkw{return} unresolved (undeclared parent); \algkw{else return} root $n$
\item \algind{1}\algkw{if} $p$ was already visited: \algkw{return} unresolved (cycle)
\item \algind{1}$n \leftarrow$ canonical identifier of $p$; $d \leftarrow d + 1$
\item \algkw{return} unresolved (chain too deep)
\end{algsteps}
\end{algorithm}
