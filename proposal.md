## 1. Title and Plain-Language Summary

**Lineage or Era? An Identifiability-Gated Variance Decomposition of Correlated Errors in
Public Language Models**

When a group of public language models is given a hard question, they often fail together
— missing the same fact, making the same mistake, reaching the same wrong answer. Two
explanations compete for this pattern of shared mistakes. The first is lineage: a model
trained on another model's outputs, or on data generated by it, tends to replicate that
model's blind spots. The second is era: models released at the same time are trained on
largely the same scrapes of the web, the same benchmark-cleansed corpora, and the same
dominant techniques, so they may pick up the same errors independently of any shared
ancestry. This project separates the two. The difficulty is that lineage and era are
tangled — descendants are always released after their ancestors, so release date is both
a cause and a confounder of the lineage path — which means the separation is only possible
on the connected subset of the open-model population where both dimensions actually vary,
and only with a statistical instrument whose estimates we first validate on simulated data
with a known answer. Our instrument partitions the observed pattern of shared errors into
a lineage-shared component, an era-shared component, and a model-specific remainder, and
the analysis is gated on the subset where that partition is identifiable. The stakes are
practical: if shared mistakes trace mostly to lineage, then diversification across model
families is a credible mitigation; if they trace mostly to era, no amount of new ancestry
helps, because every model of a given year carries the same errors.

---

## 2. Abstract

Public language models make correlated errors: when one open-weight model fails a
question, its contemporaries and descendants often fail it too. Whether this shared
failure structure is driven by lineage (a model replicating the blind spots of the models
and data it descends from) or by era (models released at the same time acquiring the same
errors through shared training data and technique) is untested, because the two are
confounded: descendants are released after their ancestors, making release year both a
mediator and a confounder of the lineage path. We propose a variance-decomposition
instrument, using the documented release record rather than reconstructed family trees,
that partitions correlated error across public models into a lineage component, an era
component, and a model-specific remainder, restricted to the connected subset of the
open-model population where lineage and era are separable. Because identifiability is the
binding constraint, the design is gated in two stages. First, a simulation study
validates that the crossed random-effects estimator recovers known ground truth under
realistic occupancy and mis-specified generative assumptions, and documents where it
fails. Second, only if the estimator survives do we decompose error responses on the
provably connected subset, with the mechanistic estimand (lineage holding era fixed)
restricted to co-released cohorts and staggered repeated fine-tunes and reported
separately from the primary observational estimand. The result decides a practical
question: whether diversifying across model families is a credible remedy for correlated
failure, or whether era-level factors dominate and render family diversification
insufficient.

## 3. Motivation and Problem Statement

### 3.1 The phenomenon: public language models fail together

Correlated error is measured, not anecdotal. Kim et al. (ICML 2025) document it across
more than 350 open and hosted models on two leaderboards and a resume-screening task:
when a pair of models both err, they agree on the wrong answer roughly 60% of the time on
one leaderboard, and the agreement persists across distinct architectures and providers
as accuracy rises. This is not cosmetic. It erodes the field's two main hedges:

- **Ensembles assume independent error.** If the shared failure of two models is caused
  by common ancestry or a common era, averaging their outputs recovers less than the
  independence assumption promises.
- **Deployment inherits blind spots.** LLM-as-judge pipelines assume a disinterested
  evaluator; Preference Leakage (Li et al., ICLR 2026) shows a judge model is measurably
  biased toward generators it is related to (same model, inheritance, or family).
  Hiring and ranking systems trained on or run by a generation of models carry whatever
  correlated errors that generation shares.

### 3.2 Two competing explanations, one confound

| Explanation | Mechanism | Testable signature |
|---|---|---|
| **Lineage** | A model replicates the blind spots of the model, outputs, and data it descends from (verified parent–offspring edges, fine-tune chains, teacher–student distillation). | Residual shared error *after* adjusting for release era, concentrated on verified lineage edges. |
| **Era** | Contemporaneous models independently acquire the same errors from shared web scrapes, benchmark-cleansed corpora, and dominant training technique. | Shared error varies by release quarter across families that share no ancestry. |

The obstacle is that the two explanations are entangled in the wild. Descendants are
always released after their ancestors, so **release year is simultaneously a mediator and
a confounder** on the lineage→error path: it is a *mediator* because lineage partly acts
by moving release forward into new data regimes; it is a *confounder* because the era's
shared data and technique impose an error structure on every contemporaneous model
regardless of ancestry. A model released in 2024Q2 shares errors with its siblings by
ancestry and with unrelated contemporaries by shared training environment. Observing
correlation does not allocate it.

### 3.3 Why the allocation is not already known

Prior work measures the pieces but never the partition:

| Work | What it measures | What it leaves open |
|---|---|---|
| Kim et al., Correlated Errors (ICML 2025) | Cross-sectional agreement across 350+ models; shared architecture/provider factors | Causality and temporality — explicitly left open by the authors |
| PhyloLM (ICLR 2025) | Family trees from output similarity; benchmark performance prediction | No variance decomposition of error traits |
| TEE, Total Evaluation Error (2026) | G-theory variance decomposition of *pipeline* facets (judge, prompt, temperature) | Decomposes measurement error, not model trait variance |
| Tracing the Roots (ACL 2026) | Dataset lineage graphs and contamination propagation | Dataset ancestry, not model error variance |
| The Subjectivity of Monoculture (2026) | Monoculture claims are null-model-dependent | Does not estimate the variance partition |
| Algorithmic Monoculture and its Critics (2026) | Evaluates objections to monoculture-as-harm | Treats correlated error as a given input; never estimates it |
| Preference Leakage (ICLR 2026) | Judge bias toward related generators | Instrument-level bias, not model trait variance |

### 3.4 The identifiability gate and the decision at stake

The allocation is identifiable only where the design is **crossed**: families spanning
multiple eras, and multiple families per era. If the population is nested (each family
confined to a single era), lineage and era are collinear and the partition is unknowable
from observational data. Phase 0 verified the open-model population is crossed, though
unbalanced and incomplete: six families, 11 of 14 release quarters (2023Q1–2026Q2) carry
two or more independent families, no family is confined to a single era, and the densest
cells are 2024Q2–2025Q2 (5–6 families per quarter). Gate: **PASS**. One limit is baked
into the population, not an identification failure: the Llama open-weights lineage
terminates at Llama 4 (Apr 2025), so post-2025 era variation is carried by the remaining
five families.

The practical decision this partition settles is **diversification-as-mitigation**. If the
lineage component dominates conditional on era, cross-family diversification is a credible
remedy for correlated failure. If the era component dominates, no amount of new ancestry
helps: the errors are a property of the era's shared data and technique, and mitigation
must diversify those instead. That fork is exactly where the intuition that "training more
independent models fixes correlated failure" disagrees with the monoculture critics —
without any evidence either way.

## 4. Precise Research Questions

One gated main question and six answerable sub-questions. Every sub-question carries an
explicit refutation condition so the program can fail cleanly (consolidated in the
Disconfirmability Register, Section 8). All questions are defined **on the connected
subset only** — where lineage and era are separable. Where the design is nested, the
partition is undefined by construction, which is a property of the design, not a data gap.

### 4.1 Main question (RQ0)

Can the observed error-correlation structure across public open-weight LMs be separated
into a lineage component and an era component — i.e., is the partition
V_lineage + V_era + V_unique estimable and non-degenerate on the connected subset?

Positive answer requires both: (a) the estimator survives Phase 1 simulation, and (b) on
real data, V_lineage|era and V_era are each estimated with non-degenerate intervals.

### 4.2 Sub-questions

| RQ | Question | Operates on | Phase | Refutation condition |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| RQ1 | Does the crossed random-effects estimator recover the true partition under known ground truth, in both a balanced-crossed (D1) and a realistic-occupancy (D2) regime? | Simulated data | 1 | Bias or collapse under D2 → estimator unfit; the real-data claim never proceeds |
| RQ2 | Under a mis-specified generative model (nested design; continuous liability thresholded to binary response), does the estimator fail *detectably* rather than silently? | Simulated data | 1 | Silent bias under mis-specification → stop program; fix or drop the instrument |
| RQ3 | On the connected subset, what share of error covariance is attributable to lineage *conditional on era*? (Primary, observational estimand.) | Connected subset of the Phase 0 population | 2 | V_lineage\|era indistinguishable from 0 → lineage refuted as a separable driver on the connected population |
| RQ4 | Holding era exactly fixed — co-released family cohorts and staggered repeated fine-tunes only — what is the structural contribution of lineage? (Secondary, mechanistic estimand.) | Co-released cohorts + staggered fine-tune chains | 2 | Reported separately, never merged into RQ3. Nonzero here with near-zero RQ3 → RQ3 is confounded by era; zero here → mechanistic lineage refuted too |
| RQ5 | After lineage adjustment, does the era component show a convergence trend across release quarters? | Connected subset | 2 | Flat trend → era-convergence refuted (register item 2; reported as a table entry, not a standalone result) |
| RQ6 | Conditional on the Phase 2 partition, is cross-family diversification a credible mitigation for correlated failure, or is era the binding constraint? | Decision layer on Phase 2 output | 2 | V_era dominates → diversification-as-remedy refuted for the connected population (register item 1) |

### 4.3 The two-estimand rule (non-negotiable)

RQ3 and RQ4 answer different questions and are never merged. Release year is both a
mediator and a confounder on the lineage→error path (Section 3.2), so the observational
primary (RQ3) adjusts for era grouping but cannot hold era fixed; the mechanistic
secondary (RQ4) is the only estimand that isolates lineage with era held fixed, and it is
available only on the small co-released and staggered-fine-tune subsets. The Phase 0
audit found just five verified cross-generation `base_model` edges, so RQ4's operating
set is deliberately small; the mechanistic claim is scoped to that set.

### 4.4 Scope exclusions

None of RQ0–RQ6 is a phylogeny-reconstruction exercise (distinct from PhyloLM), a
pipeline-measurement-error decomposition (distinct from TEE), a dataset-lineage
reconstruction (distinct from Tracing the Roots), or a welfare evaluation of monoculture
(distinct from the monoculture-critics literature). These exclusions are asserted here and
argued with the differentiation table in Section 5.

## 5. Related Work

All citations below were independently verified against the arXiv record / proceedings
before inclusion (operating rule 1). The literature measures correlated error, evaluates
its welfare consequences, and reconstructs lineage — but no existing work estimates the
partition this proposal is after.

### 5.1 Differentiation table

| Work | What it establishes | What it does not do | Differentiation from this proposal |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Kim et al., "Correlated Errors in Large Language Models"** (arXiv:2506.07962, ICML 2025, PMLR 267:30038–30066) | Agreement across 350+ open and hosted models on two leaderboards and a resume-screening task; ~60% agreement when both models err on one leaderboard; shared architecture/provider factors; agreement persists across distinct architectures and providers at higher accuracy | Causal or temporal attribution of the correlation; any variance decomposition | Same phenomenon, different question: Kim et al. document *that* correlation exists and where it concentrates; this proposal asks *how much* is lineage vs. era, on the subset where that is identifiable. Authors explicitly leave causality/temporality open |
| **PhyloLM** (Yax, Oudeyer, Palminteri; arXiv:2404.04671, ICLR 2025) | Phylogenetic (Nei-distance) trees from output similarity across open and closed models; tree distance predicts benchmark performance (MMLU, ARC, and others) | Decomposition of error variance into lineage vs. era; use of the documented release record as ground truth | PhyloLM *reconstructs* ancestry from output similarity and predicts aggregate scores; this proposal *takes* the documented release record as given and decomposes error covariance. Complementary inputs, different outputs |
| **Total Evaluation Error (TEE)** (Messing; arXiv:2604.11581) | G-theory variance decomposition + D-study of LLM evaluation *pipelines*; facets incl. judge model, temperature, prompt; item×judge interaction; under-coverage of conventional CIs | Decomposition of model error *traits* | Same estimator class (crossed random effects / REML), different grouping factors: TEE partitions measurement-pipeline facets; this proposal partitions lineage and era of the models themselves |
| **Tracing the Roots** (Li et al.; arXiv:2604.10480, ACL 2026, pp. 9606–9625) | Multi-agent reconstruction of dataset lineage in post-training; 83 seed → 430 datasets, 971 inheritance edges; contamination propagates along lineage paths | Model error variance | Dataset ancestry graphs, not model trait variance. | **The Subjectivity of Monoculture** (Jo, Garg, Raghavan; arXiv:2602.24086) | Monoculture estimates are null-model-dependent and vary with the population of models/items; increasingly expressive nulls can absorb model correlations | Variance decomposition; an estimand defined independently of a baseline | Informs our choice of decomposition (we avoid dependence on an independence null by modeling shared random effects directly), but does not estimate the partition itself |
| **Algorithmic Monoculture and its Critics** (Hedden & Raghavan; arXiv:2604.06047) | Evaluates objections to monoculture (exclusion, gaming/agency, information aggregation/exploration); concludes monoculture is less problematic than critics claim; ensemble monoculture can outperform | Any estimate of the correlated-error structure | Monoculture welfare arguments treat correlated error as a given input. This proposal supplies the input: the actual partition. If V_era dominates, the critics' "not a problem" position is materially harder to hold for the connected population |
| **Preference Leakage** (Li et al.; arXiv:2502.01534, ICLR 2026) | LLM-as-judge bias toward generators related to the judge (same model, inheritance, same family); preference-leakage score | Model error traits | Instrument-level measurement bias, a downstream *consequence* of correlated error, not its decomposition |

### 5.2 Closest neighbors

Two works deserve closer attention because a reviewer could reasonably claim the ground is
covered.

**Kim et al. (ICML 2025) is the empirical starting point, not the answer.** It measures
cross-sectional agreement across 350+ models and attributes much of it to shared
architecture and provider factors, but it is explicitly cross-sectional: the authors leave
causal and temporal decomposition open. This proposal's primary estimand is a
variance partition — a different object from an agreement rate — and its core gate (Phase
1 simulation validity under crossed/nested designs) is not addressed by any existing
correlation study.

**TEE (Messing, 2026) uses the same estimator class for a different object.** TEE applies
crossed random effects / REML (G-theory) to decompose *pipeline* measurement variance
(judge model, temperature, prompt variant). It is the strongest evidence that the
estimator class works for crossed designs in this domain — which is an argument for our
Phase 1 feasibility, not against it. The grouping factors are different: TEE's facets are
properties of the evaluation, ours are properties of the models. The two are compatible
and could eventually be composed (Section 13).

### 5.3 The gap

No verified work estimates V_lineage + V_era + V_unique for model error traits, on the
provably connected subset, with the estimator's validity established in simulation first.
The novelty claim rests on the table above: every prior work either (a) documents
correlation without partitioning it, (b) decomposes variance of the wrong object
(pipeline facets, dataset lineage), or (c) treats correlated error as a given input to a
welfare argument.

### 5.4 Terminology note

We avoid the term "convergent evolution" throughout. It is claimed by Fu et al.
(arXiv:2604.20817) for convergence in learned Fourier representations of numbers — a
different phenomenon, same term. The era channel is described with "shared training data
and technique," never "convergent evolution."

## 6. Formal Estimand & Identifiability

### 6.1 Setup and notation

Let M be the set of models in the connected subset (Phase 0 population). Each model m has
a family f(m) and a release quarter e(m); the design is family × quarter, verified crossed
but unbalanced (Phase 0). Error responses are item-level: y_mi ∈ {0,1} indicates model m
answers item i incorrectly, over a common (or comparable) item set I.

On the liability scale:

  y*_mi = δ_i + α_{f(m)} + β_{e(m)} + u_m + r_mi,   y_mi = 1{y*_mi > 0}

- δ_i — item difficulty (fixed effects).
- α_f ~ N(0, σ²_L) — lineage effect, shared by all members of family f.
- β_e ~ N(0, σ²_E) — era effect, shared by all models released in quarter e.
- u_m ~ N(0, σ²_U) — model-unique effect.
- r_mi — residual (threshold/liability error).

Because the design is crossed, the model-level variance components σ²_L, σ²_E, σ²_U are
separately estimable, and the partition on the liability scale is
σ²_L / σ²_E / σ²_U, normalized to a lineage share, era share, and model-unique share.

### 6.2 Primary estimand (observational)

  θ_P = σ²_L / (σ²_L + σ²_E + σ²_U)

The lineage share of error variance **with β_e in the model** — i.e., lineage variance
conditional on release-era grouping. This is a conditional variance share, not a causal
direct effect. Because release year is also a *mediator* on the lineage→error path
(lineage moves release forward; era imposes its error regime), the era random effect
absorbs the mediated portion, so θ_P is conservative with respect to structural lineage.
This is deliberate and stated: θ_P and θ_M bracket the truth, and neither is asserted to
be the "true" causal share.

### 6.3 Secondary estimand (mechanistic)

  θ_M = σ²_L restricted to co-released cohorts and staggered repeated fine-tunes,
       reported separately, never merged into θ_P.

On co-released cohorts, era is held fixed by construction (same quarter, different
families); on staggered fine-tune chains, lineage is held fixed while era moves. The
difference between sibling-family co-release correlation and cross-family co-release
correlation identifies lineage with era literally constant. The operating set is small —
Phase 0 verified only five cross-generation `base_model` edges — so θ_M is a scoped
structural claim on that set, not a population claim.

### 6.4 Identifiability conditions

| Condition | Status | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Crossed design: ≥2 families in ≥2 quarters, non-empty cells in both directions | VERIFIED | 6 families × 14 quarters (2023Q1–2026Q2); 11/14 quarters ≥2 independent families; no family confined to a single era |
| Not nested: within-family variation over time AND within-era variation across families | VERIFIED | Phase 0 row spans and column densities (Appendix) |
| Estimator validity under the actual (unbalanced, sparse) occupancy | TO BE SHOWN | Phase 1, D2 realistic-occupancy regime; D3 nested must fail |
| Error traits measured precisely enough | TO BE SHOWN | Item-set size and bootstrap/parametric CIs reported with every estimate |

Non-identifiability is a property of the design, not a data gap: if the population were
nested, σ²_L and σ²_E would be aliased and the partition would be unknowable from
observational data — Phase 1 D3 must reproduce exactly this failure. The lineage × era
interaction is not identified from the sparse cells and is excluded from the estimands
(Phase 1 documents the non-identifiability rather than estimating it).

### 6.5 Scope flags on the estimand

- Cross-family teacher leakage (Phi-4 trained on GPT-4o-generated data; Gemma 2 9B
  distilled from 27B; Gemma 4 built from Gemini 3) violates family independence to an
  unknown degree. It is assigned to the **era** channel (shared environment) and flagged
  as an inflation source for σ²_E, not silently left in σ²_L.
- DeepSeek V4 is a ground-up redesign, not a V3 descendant; it enters the analysis as a
  new lineage or is dropped. Within-lab generation ≠ within-lineage.

## 7. Methodology per Phase

### 7.1 Phase 1 — Simulation validation of the estimator (pre-approved plan)

**Goal:** show the crossed random-effects estimator recovers known ground truth before
any real-data claim. **Estimator:** `statsmodels.MixedLM` (REML).

| Regime | DGP | Expectation |
|---|---|---|
| D1 | Balanced crossed design, known σ²_L/σ²_E/σ²_U | Recovers ground truth (reference) |
| D2 | Occupancy copied from the Phase 0 table (unbalanced, sparse cells) | Recovers ground truth with quantified precision loss; if biased/collapsed → estimator unfit, gate fails |
| D3 | Nested design (each family confined to one era) | **Must fail detectably** (σ²_L/σ²_E aliased) |
| Liability test | Continuous liability thresholded to binary response; fit LPM-REML vs. GLMM | Decides the LPM-REML vs. GLMM path for Phase 2 |
| L×E interaction | Include lineage × era interaction in DGP | Expected unidentifiable from sparse cells; documented, not estimated |

**Deliverables:** `phase1_simulation.py`, `PHASE1_REPORT.md` (bias tables, verdicts).
**Gate:** PASS → Phase 2; silent mis-specification bias (RQ2) or D2 collapse (RQ1) → stop
program and fix or drop the instrument. No real-data claim precedes this gate.

### 7.2 Phase 2 — Real-data decomposition on the connected subset

1. Assemble item-level error responses for the connected subset models and
   **aggregate to a continuous per-model trait** (mean accuracy or IRT person
   ability over the common item set; raw item-level binary is a data source,
   not the fitted outcome — Phase 1 F4).
2. Build the family × quarter design matrix from the Phase 0 table plus parent–offspring
   edges drawn from technical reports/papers (the `base_model` field is too sparse; only
   5 verified edges).
3. Fit the Phase 1 validated estimator: **LPM-REML (CrossedREML) on the
   continuous per-model trait** (Phase 1 decision 2026-08-03). GLMM is a
   robustness check only, with the F4 era-power caveat.
4. Report the partition σ²_L / σ²_E / σ²_U with CIs as a **table**, not a headline.
   The era-convergence trend (RQ5) collapses into a table entry; it is not a standalone
   result.
5. Report θ_M separately on co-released cohorts and staggered fine-tune chains. Never
   merge θ_M into θ_P (two-estimand rule, Section 4.3).

**Deliverables:** `phase2_decomposition.py`, `PHASE2_REPORT.md`, partition table.

### 7.3 Phase 3 — Secondary analogies (gated)

Only if Phase 2 survives: heritability, selection differential, and breeder's-equation
test (Δerror-response vs. h²·S). Explicitly labeled as analogy, introduced nowhere before
this phase, and dropped if the base decomposition fails or if Δerror-response does not
track h²·S (register item 3). **Deliverable:** `phase3_report.md`.

## 13. Significance / Expected Contribution

- **If V_lineage dominates:** diversification across families is a credible mitigation;
  procurement and evaluation practice should track lineage, and family-membership
  redundancy in ensembles is quantified.
- **If V_era dominates:** diversification-as-remedy is refuted for the connected
  population (register item 1); mitigation must target the era's shared data and
  technique. This materially hardens the position of monoculture critics (Section 5.1).
- **Methodological:** a template for identifiability-gated decomposition of model
  populations, with the two-estimand structure (θ_P / θ_M) as the guard against the
  mediator–confounder collapse. Composable with TEE-style pipeline decompositions
  (Section 5.2): pipeline facets and model traits can be decomposed in the same
  framework.
- **For the monoculture literature:** supplies the missing empirical input — the actual
